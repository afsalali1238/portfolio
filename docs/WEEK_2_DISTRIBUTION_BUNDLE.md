### Staging Details:

- **Subtitle:** `AI, usage limits, and the unfinished horse: why cognitive offloading feels like genius until the rate limit hits.`

### Markdown Body:

```markdown
The first time you try it, they don’t ask you for anything.

They hand it to you with an open palm. Take as much as you want. Try it on your hardest problem.

So you take a hit.

Twenty seconds later, the brain fog is gone. It’s like the scene in _Limitless_ when the pill kicks in. Grey turns to gold.

The thoughts you’ve been fighting for weeks fall effortlessly into place. Words pour out. The answer shows up before you even finish formulating the question.

It feels like someone just wired your nervous system directly into a higher intelligence. You look at the glowing screen and think:

> ### _“I am a genius.”_

In two hours, you do what used to take two weeks. Everyone around you seems to be walking underwater.

---

### Then It Becomes Normal

The magic doesn’t stay magic for long.

Soon you need it every single morning. Two weeks of work compressed into two hours stops feeling special. It becomes your baseline. That is the new benchmark for your daily output. Nobody wrote it down; it just settled in.

---

### Then It Costs Money

The free tier runs out by 10:30 AM.

So you pull out your credit card and pay $20 a month. Fair enough. A few weeks later, you hit the rate limit again during a critical sprint. So you upgrade to $100. Then $200 across multiple API keys.

The dose didn’t get more expensive. You just need more of it to function.

---

### Then the Wall Hits: The Unfinished Horse

Everyone knows the famous drawing of the horse: the rear half is rendered in hyper-realistic, meticulous Da Vinci Renaissance detail, and the front half degrades into a childish stick figure scribbled with a dying crayon.

That is what happens to your work the moment the red banner appears: `You have reached your usage limit. Resets at 3:00 PM.`

Suddenly, the prosthetic brain is unplugged. You sit at your desk staring at an unfinished document, and you realize with sudden panic: _you have forgotten how to think without the accelerator._

Your unassisted brain feels heavy, sluggish, and easily distracted. You can’t even write a crisp three-line email without wanting to prompt a model for tone.

> **If the model is doing the synthesis, the model is building the muscle. When you delegate all struggle, you delegate the intellect.**

---

### The Operator’s Rule of Bounded Leverage

AI is the most astounding cognitive lever ever constructed. But like any lever, if you lean your full body weight on it without keeping your own feet planted on the floor, the moment it snaps, you smash your face on the ground.

I use frontier AI daily across logistics, clinical translation tools, and systems. But I maintain strict operating hygiene:

1. **Never prompt what you cannot verify.** If you don’t understand the output deeply enough to spot a subtle hallucination in production, you have no business shipping it.
2. **Do the first 20 minutes in analog.** Structure the problem in your own journal or whiteboard before touching the keyboard.
3. **Maintain unassisted reps.** Write long-form thoughts by hand. Debate with real operators in the room. If you can’t hold the logic in your head, the software will collapse under you.

The first hit is free. The second hit costs twenty dollars. The third hit costs your attention. Use the tool with ruthless clarity — but never surrender your judgment.
```
